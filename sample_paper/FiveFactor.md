# A Five-Factor Asset Pricing Model

## Authors
Eugene F. Fama, Kenneth R. French

## Keywords
Asset pricing model, Factor model, Dividend discount model

## 1. Core Contribution & Objective
The paper proposes a five-factor asset pricing model to better explain the patterns in average stock returns, particularly focusing on size, value, profitability, and investment factors. It aims to address the limitations of the three-factor model by adding profitability and investment factors.

## 2. Methodology & Framework
The authors use a five-factor model to test its performance against the three-factor model of Fama and French (1993) and other models. They employ various factor definitions and test the model on different portfolios formed based on size, book-to-market equity ratio (B/M), profitability, and investment. The analysis is conducted using monthly returns data from 1963 to 2013.

## 3. Key Findings & Data Insights
The five-factor model performs better than the three-factor model in explaining the average returns on portfolios formed based on size, B/M, profitability, and investment. Specifically, the five-factor model reduces the negative intercepts for portfolios of small stocks with negative exposures to profitability and investment factors. However, the model still leaves a significant unexplained variance in returns, particularly for small stocks with high investment and low profitability.

## 4. Limitations & Future Work
The authors note that the five-factor model is still not a complete description of expected returns, as it leaves a significant unexplained variance. They also mention that the performance of the model is sensitive to the specific definitions of the factors and the way they are constructed.

## 5. Terms
- **Five-factor model**: A model that includes five factors: market return, size, value, profitability, and investment, to explain average stock returns.
- **HML**: The value factor, calculated as the difference between the returns of high B/M and low B/M portfolios.
- **RMW**: The profitability factor, calculated as the difference between the returns of robust and weak profitability portfolios.
