# A five-factor asset pricing model

## Authors
Eugene F. Fama, Kenneth R. French

## Keywords
Asset pricing model, Factor model, Profitability

## 1. Core Contribution & Objective
Addresses the limitations of the three-factor Fama-French (FF) model by incorporating profitability and investment factors to explain average stock returns. The core hypothesis is that adding profitability (RMW) and investment (CMA) factors improves model performance and renders the value factor (HML) redundant for describing average returns in the examined sample.

## 2. Methodology & Framework
Uses monthly excess return data from NYSE, AMEX, and NASDAQ stocks (CRSP and Compustat) from July 1963 to December 2013. Constructs five-factor models using three sets of factor definitions: separate sorts on Size and B/M/OP/Inv (2 & 3), separate sorts with two groups (2 & 2), and joint controls (2 & 2 & 2 & 2). Employs time-series regressions to test model performance via GRS statistics, intercepts, and factor exposures.

## 3. Key Findings & Data Insights
The five-factor model reduces unexplained return variance compared to the three-factor model (Table 5). HML is found to be redundant as its average return is captured by exposures to RMW and CMA, with near-zero intercepts in regressions of HML on other factors (Table 6). Small stocks with high investment and low profitability remain a major anomaly with negative five-factor intercepts (Tables 7-11). The model explains between 71% and 94% of the cross-section variance of expected returns.

## 4. Limitations & Future Work
Measurement error inflates average absolute intercept estimates. The redundancy of HML may be specific to US data from 1963–2013. Joint controls in factor construction can lead to poor diversification for some portfolios (e.g., highly profitable extreme value stocks).

## 5. Terms
- **SMB**: Size factor, representing the return difference between small and large stocks.
- **HML**: Value factor, representing the return difference between high and low book-to-market equity stocks.
- **RMW**: Profitability factor, representing the return difference between firms with robust and weak operating profitability.
