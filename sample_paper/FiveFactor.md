# Conclusions

## Authors
Eugene F. Fama, Kenneth R. French

## Keywords
size, value, profitability, investment, five-factor model, asset pricing

## 1. Core Contribution & Objective
The authors present evidence that portfolios of small stocks with negative exposures to RMW and CMA are the biggest asset pricing problem in four of the six sets of LHS portfolios examined. They also suggest that HML is a redundant factor in the five-factor model, and that a four-factor model that drops HML performs as well as the five-factor model in applications where the sole interest is abnormal returns (measured by regression intercepts). However, if one is also interested in portfolio tilts toward Size, value, profitability, and investment premiums, the five-factor model is the choice.

## 2. Methodology & Framework
The authors use independent sorts to form two or three groups of stocks based on size, profitability, and investment, and then use these groups to construct factors. They estimate a five-factor model and compare its performance to a four-factor model that drops HML, and find that the four-factor model performs as well as the five-factor model in applications where the sole interest is abnormal returns (measured by regression intercepts).

## 3. Key Findings & Data Insights
1. Portfolios of small stocks with negative exposures to RMW and CMA are the biggest asset pricing problem in four of the six sets of LHS portfolios examined. 2. HML is a redundant factor in the five-factor model, and a four-factor model that drops HML performs as well as the five-factor model in applications where the sole interest is abnormal returns (measured by regression intercepts). 3. If one is also interested in portfolio tilts toward Size, value, profitability, and investment premiums, the five-factor model is the choice.

## 4. Limitations & Future Work
The authors note that the joint controls of the 2&2&2&2 sorts are attractive for isolating estimates of factor premiums, but controlling for more factors is problematic, as it may result in poor diversification of some of the portfolios used to construct factors. Additionally, the choice between the 2&3 and 2&2 sorts is inconsequential for the LHS portfolios examined.

## 5. Terms
- **HML**: High Minus Low factor, which measures the difference in returns between high and low book-to-market value stocks.
- **RMW**: Robust Minus Weak factor, which measures the difference in returns between robust and weak profitability stocks.
- **CMA**: Conservative Minus Aggressive factor, which measures the difference in returns between conservative and aggressive investment stocks.
- **SMB**: Small Minus Big factor, which measures the difference in returns between small and big stocks.
- **five-factor model**: A model that includes the market factor, size factor, value factor, profitability factor, and investment factor.
