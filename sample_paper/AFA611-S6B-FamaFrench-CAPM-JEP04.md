# The Capital Asset Pricing Model: Theory and Evidence

## Authors
- Eugene F. Fama
- Kenneth R. French

## Keywords
- Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)
- Asset Pricing Theory
- Empirical Tests
- Market Beta
- Expected Returns

## 1. Core Contribution & Objective
The paper aims to review and critique the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM), which was introduced by William Sharpe and John Lintner in the 1960s. The authors document the empirical failures of the CAPM and discuss alternative models that better explain the cross-section of expected stock returns. The core objective is to evaluate the CAPM's validity and its applicability in financial practice, particularly in estimating the cost of equity and evaluating mutual fund performance.

## 2. Methodology & Framework
The authors present a detailed review of empirical tests conducted on the CAPM, including both cross-section and time-series regressions. They also discuss the limitations of the CAPM, such as the unrealistic assumptions about investor behavior and market efficiency. The framework involves testing the CAPM using various market proxies and price ratios to identify factors that explain the variation in expected returns beyond market beta.

## 3. Key Findings & Data Insights
- **Empirical Rejections of the CAPM**: The CAPM fails to explain the cross-section of expected returns, particularly for factors like size, book-to-market equity, and momentum.
- **Three-Factor Model**: The authors propose a three-factor model that includes market beta, size, and book-to-market equity factors to better explain expected returns.
- **Market Proxy Problem**: The use of market proxies in empirical tests often leads to rejections of the CAPM, suggesting that the true market portfolio is elusive and difficult to proxy accurately.
- **Momentum Effect**: The momentum effect, which is distinct from the value effect, is not explained by the three-factor model or the CAPM.

## 4. Limitations & Future Work
- **Market Portfolio Elusiveness**: The true market portfolio is difficult to define and proxy, leading to limitations in empirical tests.
- **Behavioral Explanations**: Behavioral finance theories suggest that investor overreaction and mispricing could explain the empirical contradictions of the CAPM.
- **Further Research**: The authors call for more research to develop models that can better capture the complexities of asset pricing, including the role of information and investor behavior.

## 5. Definitions or Key Terms
- **Market Portfolio**: The portfolio of all available assets, weighted by their market value.
- **Market Beta**: The covariance of an asset's return with the market return, standardized by the variance of the market return.
- **Three-Factor Model**: A model that includes market beta, size, and book-to-market equity factors to explain expected returns.
