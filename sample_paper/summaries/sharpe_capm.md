# Capital Asset Prices: A Theory of Market Equilibrium under Conditions of Risk

## Authors
William F. Sharpe

## Keywords
Capital Asset Pricing Model, Market Equilibrium, Systematic Risk

## Short Title
Capital Asset Pricing Model Theory

## Abstract
This foundational paper derives the equilibrium relationship between expected asset returns and systematic risk. It establishes that in a diversified market, only non-diversifiable risk is priced, leading to the Security Market Line.

## 1. Core Contribution & Objective
Solves the problem of asset pricing under uncertainty by proving that expected return is linearly related to beta (systematic risk), not total variance.

## 2. Methodology & Framework
Theoretical derivation utilizing mean-variance analysis and portfolio theory assumptions within a general equilibrium framework.

## 3. Key Findings & Data Insights
Derivation of the Security Market Line equation; demonstration that idiosyncratic risk is eliminated through diversification and thus does not command a risk premium.

## 4. Limitations & Future Work
Relies on assumptions of homogeneous expectations, frictionless markets, single-period horizon, and rational investors with quadratic utility functions.

## 5. Terms
- **CAPM**: Capital Asset Pricing Model, the relationship between expected return and systematic risk.
- **Beta**: A coefficient measuring an asset's volatility relative to the overall market.
- **Systematic Risk**: Market-wide risk that affects all assets and cannot be diversified away.
