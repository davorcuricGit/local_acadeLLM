# The Capital Asset Pricing Model: Theory and Evidence

## Authors
Eugene F. Fama, Kenneth R. French

## Keywords
Asset Pricing, CAPM, Empirical Tests

## Short Title
CAPM Theory and Evidence

## Abstract
This paper reviews the theoretical foundations and empirical evidence surrounding the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM). It outlines the logic of the model, discusses early empirical tests that rejected the Sharpe-Lintner version, and synthesizes evidence on anomalies such as size and book-to-market effects. The authors argue that while the CAPM is theoretically elegant, its empirical failures invalidate most practical applications.

## 1. Core Contribution & Objective
The paper synthesizes decades of empirical research to demonstrate that the CAPM's core predictions regarding risk and expected return are inconsistent with observed market data. It argues that the model's assumptions (e.g., complete agreement on distributions, unrestricted borrowing/lending) are unrealistic and that alternative variables (size, value) significantly explain returns beyond beta.

## 2. Methodology & Framework
Review of literature and analysis of historical stock return data from CRSP (1928-2003). The authors utilize cross-section and time-series regression analyses to test CAPM predictions against observed portfolio returns.

## 3. Key Findings & Data Insights
- Early tests reject Sharpe-Lintner CAPM: intercepts exceed risk-free rates, beta slopes are too flat. - Size and Value effects: Small stocks and high book-to-market stocks yield higher returns than predicted by beta alone. - Three-Factor Model (Fama-French) outperforms CAPM in explaining average returns. - Market Proxy Problem: Standard proxies (e.g., US common stocks) fail to capture the true market portfolio, leading to invalid test results.

## 4. Limitations & Future Work
The paper notes that standard market proxies are likely invalid for testing the model. It acknowledges an unresolved debate between behavioral explanations (irrational pricing) and rational risk-based explanations (state variables). The authors also highlight large standard errors in estimating market premiums and betas.

## 5. Terms
- **CAPM**: Capital Asset Pricing Model, a theoretical framework relating expected asset returns to systematic risk (beta).
- **Beta**: A measure of an asset's sensitivity to market movements, used as a proxy for systematic risk in the CAPM.
- **Three-Factor Model**: An extension of the CAPM that includes size (SMB) and value (HML) factors alongside the market factor to explain stock returns.
